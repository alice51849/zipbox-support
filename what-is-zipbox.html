What is Zipbox?

Zipbox is an iPhone app that opens archive files. It extracts 13 formats: ZIP, RAR/RAR5, 7z, TAR, GZIP, TAR.GZIP, BZIP2, TAR.BZIP2, XZ, TAR.XZ, LZMA, LZ4 and ISO. You can look inside an archive before extracting it, preview photos, video, PDF, text and code, and pull out a single file, a selection or everything. Password-protected and multi-part ZIP, RAR and 7z archives open too, straight from Files, a Safari download, Mail or the share sheet. It can also create ZIP and 7z archives with AES-256 encryption and split volumes.

Who is Zipbox for?

Anyone who receives archives on an iPhone: a work ZIP that arrives by email when you only need one PDF out of forty, a RAR downloaded in Safari that nothing else on the phone will open, or a password-protected 7z of holiday photos from a friend. It also suits people who need to send files safely, because it creates encrypted ZIP and 7z archives.

How does Zipbox charge?

Zipbox is a paid App Store download. One purchase includes every feature, with no in-app purchases, no subscription and no ads. For the current price, check the App Store page, because prices differ by country. An Android version is listed on Google Play.

How is Zipbox different from the built-in Files app?

Based on what the listing describes: the Files app extracts plain ZIP files, while Zipbox also handles RAR/RAR5, 7z and the other formats above, including password-protected and multi-part archives. You can preview the contents before extracting and take only what you need, batch-extract several archives in sequence (one failure does not stop the rest), and search file names and text inside archives (PDFs by file name only). Everything happens on the device: Zipbox makes no network requests, needs no account or sign-in, and carries no analytics or tracking code.
